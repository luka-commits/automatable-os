Freelancer OS

Finding the work and doing the work, in one place.

Most freelance tooling stops at the lead. This one carries a job from the search that found it through to the project you deliver, and it never sends anything without you. Below is every step, what happens inside it, and why it is shaped that way.

Three layers, and each works without the ones above it

You can use the day loop and never connect an Upwork account.

1

The day

morning opens it, eod closes it, ingest files what comes in. Tasks live in one file, projects in another, and a static dashboard renders both.

Stands alone. Needs no Upwork, no Google account, no key.
2

Acquisition

Finds jobs worth your time, scores them against your own niche rather than a generic one, and turns a good one into something you can send.

Needs the Upwork connector. Skip it entirely and layers 1 and 3 are unaffected.
3

Delivery

A won job becomes a project with its own folder, its own first task and the proposal moved in as material. From there it is ordinary work that layer 1 already carries.

Works for referrals and direct clients too. Upwork is one way in, not the only one.

The day is a loop, not a list

Two commands bracket it. Everything in between is ordinary work.

  1. morningwhat today is
  3. the workskills, on demand
  5. eodwhat happened

and round again: what you decided today is what tomorrow starts from

morningCalendar, mail, tasks and the pipeline, in one pass
What happens
Reads your calendar and mail if they are connected, sorts the mail by what needs an answer, then works through your open tasks and gives them to you one at a time rather than as a list. If Upwork is set up, it makes one pass over the pipeline: new matches, anything a client replied to, follow-ups due today.
Why it works this way
A flat list of twenty tasks gets re-sorted by you every single morning, and that sorting is real work you redo daily. So the tasks arrive ordered: urgent and important first, then important, then merely urgent. You can disagree with the order in one sentence and it gets corrected in the file, not argued about.
Without mail or calendar
It still runs, still briefs you on tasks and the pipeline, and names in one line what it skipped. A missing tool degrades a step; it never fails the run.
eodThe plan against what actually happened
What happens
Compares what you planned against what got done, writes three to five lines into the journal, moves finished tasks out, and names what tomorrow starts with.
Why it works this way
What you did is recoverable from the files and the git history. What you decided, and why, is not, and in six months that is the only thing you actually want back. A journal entry that records the what and skips the why is a changelog, and you already have one of those.
ingestSomething arrived. Where does it go?
What happens
A document, a transcript, a voice note or a link gets read, summarised, and filed into the right project. Into inputs/ when the client sent it, into work/ when it is yours.
Why it works this way
The question that arrives three months later is "what exactly did the client send me". It is unanswerable if everything landed in one pile. The split costs nothing at filing time and is the whole value later.

Acquisition, in the order it actually happens

Every step here drafts. None of them sends.

upwork-profileBefore the first application, not after the tenth
What happens
Audits the profile you have (title, overview, skills, rate, portfolio, video) and hands back finished text to paste in, with what each weakness costs you. No profile yet, and it walks you through building one.
Why it comes first
The profile decides whether you appear in search results at all, and it is the first thing a client opens after your cover letter. Applying around a weak profile makes every single proposal more expensive than it needs to be.
An honest limit
Upwork's API can write availability, employment, languages and education, and nothing else. Title, overview, skills, rate and portfolio, exactly the set that matters, have to be pasted in by hand. So the skill hands you text rather than promising a change it cannot make.
upwork-screenerFinds jobs and scores them against your niche
What happens
Searches your own tracks, pages through the results, and scores each job against context/expertise.md: what a strong match looks like for you, and the traps that look right and are not. Everything lands in the pipeline with a score and a reason.
Why the paging matters
The API returns ten results per call and has no date filter. On a busy search term ten recency-sorted results cover a few hours, so a once-daily single-page run misses most of what was posted, and misses it invisibly. Paging is not thoroughness here, it is correctness.
Why your own traps
A generic scorer rates everything alike. The one thing that makes it yours is a real job you turned down or regretted taking, and the reason behind it. That one example is worth more than a page of criteria.
upwork-pitch-pageA one-page site for a single job
What happens
Builds a self-contained page for one specific job: an interactive diagram of the solution the client can pan and edit, a slot for your walkthrough video, your real numbers, and real reviews. One file you can host or send.
Why a page and not a longer proposal
A cover letter asks the client to imagine your plan. A page lets them look at it. For a job worth a real budget that difference is the whole application.
When to build the thing instead
If the job names a tool that can be driven programmatically, the posting is clear enough that building is not guesswork, and the budget justifies the extra hour: build it and link it. A landing page gets deployed and sent as a live URL. A gig-shop posting gets a diagram.
upwork-proposalThe cover letter you actually send
What happens
Reads the full posting, including the screening instruction that hides in the description and never appears in any field, then writes the letter and shows it to you and stops.
Why it reads your old proposals
During setup the system reads the complete text of proposals you have already sent. That is your writing, in the right register, on the right platform, already approved by you once. Writing in that shape beats writing in a generic one, and you did not have to type a style guide.
upwork-inbox & upwork-replyThe client answered
What happens
One call returns the whole account picture: unread rooms, invitations, offers, contracts, connects. Anything that is genuinely an action gets a draft reply in three different directions: say yes, ask the one question that unblocks it, propose a time. Nothing is sent.
Why three and not ten
Ten versions of the same message is noise you have to read. Three real directions is a choice you can make in five seconds.
What it refuses to guess
The API does not say who wrote a message: yours and the client's are indistinguishable in the transcript. So the unread count is the signal that the client wrote, and any attribution beyond that is stated as an inference rather than a fact.
upwork-wonThe handover from pipeline to work
What happens
Creates the project folder from the job record, writes its first task, moves the proposal and the pitch page in as material, and links the job back to the project so the funnel still counts it.
Why this step exists at all
Without it everything above is a lead generator. The job sits in the pipeline with a final status while the client, the deadlines and the deliverables live nowhere. This is the step that makes the rest an operating system.
The one part no script can do
The Upwork message thread cannot be exported, and it is the most valuable document in the project: the posting says what they asked for, the thread says what you agreed to. So the skill offers to read it and write the decisions down: a summary of what was promised, not a transcript.

The rail everything runs inside

Nothing leaves your account without you saying so, in that moment

Not a proposal, not a message, not an offer response. Drafting, scoring, ranking and preparing are automatic. The last step is always yours, and this is not caution for its own sake. Upwork names these as grounds for suspension:

"Using OAuth2 tokens or session cookies from a browser or an official client in a script or bot"
"Exceeding rate limits or running background polling that resembles scraping"

So there is no timer, no loop, no hosted runner, and no token ever leaves the machine. An approved API key would permit automation, and it requires $25,000 in lifetime earnings and a 90% Job Success Score. Until then the human in front of the screen is the compliance mechanism, and the account is the thing worth protecting.

Nothing here is mandatory. Not even Upwork.

A system that demands eight accounts before it does anything gets abandoned at account three.

A missing tool degrades a step

morning without a mail connection still briefs you on your calendar and tasks, and names what it skipped. The screener without Slack writes to the dashboard instead of sending a message. Neither stops, and neither pretends.

A preference is never a requirement

Terminal, editor, task manager, note app: you already have those. Everything here reads from and writes to plain files, so you keep your tools. Anything that would be installed gets asked about first, and no is a complete answer.

No Upwork, no dead weight

Say you do not work on Upwork during setup and the whole acquisition layer stands down: no connector, no nagging, no half-empty tab. The day loop, the projects and the dashboard are unaffected.

The dashboard is a pure view

It is generated from your files and never hand-edited. Every button copies a ready sentence to your clipboard; it never writes state itself. One source of truth, and no second one quietly drifting from it.

Where things live

Four places, and the split between them is the one rule worth learning.

PlaceWhat it holdsWhy there
context/ Your tasks, your projects' status, the journal, your niche and your background State lives in exactly one place. The question you ask each morning is "how does everything stand", never "how does one project stand alone"
projects/ One folder per client engagement: what they sent, what you are making, what you delivered Material lives with its project. "What did the client send" is always asked from inside one project
reference/ The handbooks and the scripts. What each tool can do, what Upwork allows, how the dashboard is built Read on demand, so a skill can link to one settled answer instead of restating it
.claude/skills/ The commands themselves Each one encodes something learned the hard way, which is why they are read rather than improvised around

Every file that becomes yours is gitignored and ships as <name>.example. That is what makes git pull safe: the machinery updates, your data does not move.
