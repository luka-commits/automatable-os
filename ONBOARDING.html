Freelancer OS · Onboarding

What the first run actually does.

One conversation, not a form, and roughly half an hour. Below is every phase it goes through, which tools get connected and why, and where the keys end up. Nothing is installed or connected before you have said yes to it.

The five phases

Started by a session-start hook on your first message. You do not have to know a command to begin.

setupRequired

Who you are, and what you are working on

Language first, then six questions. The one that matters most is the fifth: which tools you work with every day, and what each one is for. Everything in phase 3 is derived from that answer rather than from a checklist.

  • Your name, how you want to be addressed, your working language
  • Whether this folder is empty or already holds work
  • Your running projects, one sentence each
  • Your daily tools, and what each is for
Leaves behind context/config.yaml, STATUS.md, PROJECTS.md, and a folder per project

Your existing material gets filed

Documents you already have are read, summarised and put where they belong: what a client sent into inputs/, what you made into work/. If the folder already had a structure, it is measured first and you are asked before anything moves.

Leaves behind your documents inside the right project, and nothing deleted

The equipment

The part that otherwise never happens. Four rounds, and each is one question per group rather than one per tool, because six questions in a row get answered with "yes, yes, yes", which is not consent.

  • Tools installed on your machine
  • Connections to the systems you named in phase 1
  • Keys, for the tools that are half dead without one
  • Repos, cloned into the project they belong to
All four are broken out in the tables below
setup-freelancer-osUpwork only

The acquisition channel

One question: do you work on Upwork, or would you like to start. Three answers, and they lead three different ways.

  • Yes → the connector is verified and your account is read: profile, past contracts, and the full text of proposals you have already sent. Then three questions, because they are the only ones no API can answer
  • Starting out → nothing to read, so it asks instead, and upwork-profile builds your profile rather than auditing it
  • No → the whole layer stands down and is never mentioned again
Leaves behind expertise.md, experience.md and your Upwork ids, or upwork_enabled: false
morningRequired

The first day starts

The setup skill archives itself away, so it can never run twice. Nothing is left to configure: the briefing reads what the phases above wrote and hands you your tasks one at a time.

Leaves behind BRIEFING.md and a dashboard you open on a double click

A connector that does not exist gets said out loud

The catalogue moves in both directions, so a route is never guessed at. If your system is not in it you hear that plainly, along with what stays possible, rather than being walked into a dead end. A connection that fails twice is recorded as not set up and the setup carries on: the rest does not wait for it.

Nothing lands on your machine before you have heard what it is and what it does for you. One question per group, and no is a complete answer that gets recorded so nobody asks twice.

Where the keys go

Into ~/.config/credentials.env, outside this folder, always.

Never into the repo, and the reason is not caution: this folder gets cloned and versioned, and a key committed once stays in the history even after you delete the file. Outside the folder there is nothing to clean up later.

KeyWhy
FirecrawlOffered Without it the tool is installed and cannot read a page. Your own account, the free tier is enough to try it
OpenRouterOffered Images, and models that do not come from Anthropic. Without it the system says so honestly rather than failing at the moment you ask
Anthropic APIOn request Only the managed-agents skill needs one, and it bills separately from your subscription. Never offered unprompted for that reason

And one repo, which is your backup

The setup offers to create a private GitHub repo under your account, and the end of each day pushes the state of this folder into it. That is the one thing that ever leaves your machine, it belongs to you, and nobody else is offered access to it.

Project repos are separate: if a project has one, it is cloned into projects/<slug>/code, so its history stays its own instead of being mixed into the workspace.

What happens after the setup, every day, is on SYSTEM.html. The same path in prose, with the reasoning behind each step, is ONBOARDING.md. No external fonts and no network calls, so this works on a double click, offline.
