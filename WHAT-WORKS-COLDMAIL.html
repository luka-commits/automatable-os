How many mailboxes: arithmetic, not a guess

Instantly caps a mailbox at 30 campaign mails a day. You scale by adding mailboxes across more domains, never by sending more from each.

How the market gets scraped

You name a trade and a country. Everything below happens once, takes a few hours, and costs about five dollars. These are the real numbers from the UK locksmith run.

Why 46 separate scrapes

One search on Google Maps returns a few dozen results, however many exist. So the country is walked one county at a time — that is the difference between a few hundred businesses and four thousand.

Why the chains go

Google lists one row per shop. Timpson had 1,877 of them, all the same company and the same website. Mailing them all is not weak personalisation, it is spam.

Why some get a second look

A hardware store that mostly cuts keys has the wrong category on Google. Dropping it for that reason loses a real customer, so anything ambiguous is checked rather than binned.

Where the emails come from

Seven in ten are on the profile itself. For the rest, their website's homepage and contact page get read — and only for the ones missing one, because each read costs.

The twelve things it scores

Every Google profile in the market gets the same twelve questions. The weight says how much each one moves the number — they are not equal, because Google does not treat them as equal either.

A question we could not answer is not a failure. If the scrape did not return a field, that factor leaves the sum entirely — out of the top and the bottom of the fraction. It is the reason the score can be put in front of the recipient at all: he is never marked down for a gap in our data. Below seven measured factors there is no score at all, because a number built on five looks exactly like one built on twelve.

Two obvious ones are deliberately missing: whether the profile is claimed, and whether it has a phone number. 99% of businesses have both, so scoring them moves every number up by the same amount and separates nobody. They appear in the mail's opening list instead, where they belong — as things this business already has right.

Then it goes out

The last step, and the only one that touches the outside world. It is two commands rather than one, on purpose.

Tracking stays off

A tracking pixel is one of the fastest routes into a spam folder, and knowing who opened a mail buys a freelancer nothing he can act on.

It stops on a reply

Nobody gets the follow-up after they have already answered. Obvious, and the default in most tools is the opposite.

Eight days, not one

2,483 mails at 300 a day. The limit is not a setting to raise — it is what keeps the mailboxes alive.

Nothing leaves without you

Uploading and starting are separate commands. That is a promise the base layer makes for every add-on, and this one keeps it.

What the machine does

Every business in the market, scored against every other one. Then each mail written out of what was measured — and the ones that cannot be backed up thrown away.

The whole market, not a sample

He knows he has 64 reviews. That the median for his trade is 20 is the part he cannot look up anywhere.

Unmeasured never counts against him

A factor the scrape did not return leaves the sum entirely. That one rule is why the score can be put in front of him at all.

The same business, the same mail

Wording is chosen by his Google ID, not at random. One sent three weeks ago can still be reproduced exactly.

No model, and that is not nostalgia

A model can invent a number that is not in the data. This cannot — every figure has to exist in that business's own sheet.

What it will not and cannot do

Each limit is there because the alternative costs more than it gives.

It does not send by itself

Uploading the campaign is a separate command and needs your yes. An add-on does not get to break a promise the base made on its behalf.

Local businesses with a Google profile

Trades, clinics, salons — anyone whose customers find them on a map. If your buyers are SaaS founders, this add-on has nothing to measure.

It will not invent a number

Every figure has to exist in that business's data sheet or the mail is thrown out. A lead with too little data gets fewer points, or none.

It cannot make a bad offer work

It gets a personal, well-built mail into the right inbox at a volume you could not reach by hand. What you sell is still yours.
