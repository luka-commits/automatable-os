# Worked example: a two-brand CRM build

An invented job and an invented freelancer, close enough to a real posting to be useful. Read it
for the **shape**, not the content: your numbers, your tools and your voice replace all of it.

**The posting:** one sales team handling enquiries for two separate brands with separate
websites. They want both in one CRM, want to know which brand and which ad campaign produced
each lead, and want follow-up to happen without anyone remembering to do it. 20-30 hours,
$12-25/hr, and the description asks applicants to open with a specific phrase.

**Worth knowing before you copy the shape: the first draft of this failed its own rule.** Seven
delivery lines, only four carried a number. Lines 2, 4 and 5 were rewritten to quantify. That is
the normal failure mode, not an unusual one, which is why `check_proposal.py` exists.

---

## The letter

> Two Brands CRM Specialist
>
> Hi Sam, I have built this exact setup twice: two brands, one sales team, one CRM, without the
> two lead sources bleeding into each other. In both cases the sales team stopped asking "where
> did this one come from" within the first week.
>
> Here's what I'll deliver for your project:
>
> 1. One CRM environment carrying both brands, with every lead tagged by brand at the point of
>    entry, so no report ever needs manual sorting.
> 2. Both website forms wired in, preserving UTM, Google Ads and Meta parameters through to the
>    contact record, so attribution survives the handoff.
> 3. A 7-stage pipeline from new lead to won or lost, matching the stages your team already
>    uses verbally.
> 4. Automatic assignment by brand and availability, with a notification inside 60 seconds of a
>    new enquiry.
> 5. A follow-up sequence covering the first 14 days: confirmation, two nudges, and a task for
>    the rep if nobody has made contact within 24 hours.
> 6. Email and SMS configured end to end, including the DNS records and the consent handling
>    that keeps you out of spam folders.
> 7. Full test pass on both brands before handover, plus a 15-minute recorded walkthrough your
>    team can rewatch instead of asking me.
>
> Here's a walkthrough of how I would build this, using your two sites as the example:
> [INSERT LOOM LINK HERE]
>
> I work in milestones rather than a lump sum, so you approve each phase before we move on.
>
> **Here's why I'm the right fit:**
>
> ## Two brands, one sales team
>
> This is the part most setups get wrong, and it is not a tagging problem. It is a routing
> problem: the same rep sees both, so the CRM has to make the brand obvious at a glance and in
> every report, without doubling the workload.
>
> ## Attribution that survives the form
>
> Campaign parameters are usually lost between the ad click and the contact record. I carry them
> through as hidden fields and verify with a live test from each ad platform, so "which campaign
> produced this" is a filter rather than a guess.
>
> ## Follow-up that runs without anyone remembering
>
> The sequence is the easy half. The reliable half is the fallback task when a human does not
> act, which is what actually stops leads going quiet.
>
> Let's hop on a 15-minute call to scope this out. When works for you?
>
> Thanks for your consideration. Looking forward to hearing from you.
>
> Best,
> [your first name]

## What it is doing, mapped to the steps

| Part | Where it shows | The point |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Proof up front | "built this exact setup twice", the outcome sentence | The client skims. Proof before promises. |
| 2. Seven quantified lines | every one carries a number or a countable thing | "0 manual sorting", "inside 60 seconds", "7 stages", "14 days" |
| 3. Video line | present, still a placeholder | The letter never waits for the recording |
| 4. Risk reversal | milestone framing | Costs nothing to promise, and is how fixed-price works anyway |
| 5. Requirement mirrors | three `##` headers | Their words, not your categories. Three, never more. |
| 6. CTA | one specific action | A question they can answer in five words |
| 7. Close | plain, no flourish | |

**Two things this example does not do, on purpose.** It claims no badge, because the invented
freelancer has not earned one. And it does not open with the phrase the posting demanded, since
that phrase is different every time. **Read the full description before sending** — a screening
instruction buried in paragraph nine is the most common way a good proposal gets discarded
unread.
