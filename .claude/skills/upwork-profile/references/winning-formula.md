# The formula behind profiles that win

Extracted 14.08.2026 from two sources that agree with each other: **three real top-earning
profiles** pulled through the MCP, and **Jono Catliff's publicly published bio and proposal
formulas** (his "$1M+ Upwork bio formula").

Where they agree, treat it as the default. Where a single profile does something unusual,
it is an option, not a rule.

## The three profiles this is measured against

| | Title | Rate | Earnings | Jobs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Email Marketing & Automation \| Meta / Facebook Ads \| Landing Pages | $75 | $956,507 | 734, Top Rated Plus |
| B | ClickFunnels Expert & Facebook Ads Specialist \| GoHighLevel | $97 | $295,146 | 91 |
| C | AI Agents & Automation \| Full Stack \| CRM/ERP Integration \| DevOps | $95 | $1,088,697 | 475, 100% JSS, Top Rated Plus |

Three different niches, three different rates, one shared structure — which is the reason
the patterns below are treated as the default rather than as one person's taste.

**Why they are unnamed here.** Their profiles are public, but a public profile is not the
same as a compiled list of named people with their earnings sitting in a repo anyone can
clone. The formula is what transfers; the identities add nothing to it. Run
`upwork-profile` and it pulls three current top earners in **your** niche live, which is
both fresher and more relevant than any list frozen into this file.

## What all three do

**1. The title is keywords separated by pipes, never a sentence.** Three or four blocks, each
one a thing a client would actually type into search. No "helping businesses grow", no job
title alone. Every one of the three is built this way, and the pattern survives across three
completely different niches.

**2. The first line states an outcome or a hard number. Never "I am a…".** This is the line
that decides whether the rest gets read, because everything past roughly 250 characters hides
behind "more".

- A opens on the outcome: "I help businesses increase revenue by improving customer acquisition, conversion rates, and retention."
- B opens on ownership plus proof: "When you hire me I take responsibility for the profitability of your ADS and FUNNELS. $10M+ in sales generated. 700+ funnels delivered. 300+ clients served."
- C opens on the differentiator: one technical partner "instead of clients juggling 3-4 specialists who blame each other when systems break."

**3. Proof is front-loaded, and the strongest proof goes first.** Catliff's tier ranking, which
matches what these three actually put at the top:

| Tier | What counts |
|---|---|
| **S — closers** | video testimonials · detailed case studies with hard numbers · Top Rated · 100% JSS · $100K+ earned on Upwork |
| **A — strong support** | Top 1% for a skill · major press · 100+ reviews · 100+ projects · spoke at events |
| **B — outcome numbers** | hours saved · client revenue earned · named big-brand work · 8-figure company work |
| **C — nice to have** | certifications · years of experience · following · systems built |

C leads with a block of S and A tier in one breath: $1M+ earnings, 462 projects, 16,635
hours, 100% JSS, Top Rated Plus, certified partner of Monday/Make/Vapi/Retell/Palantir.

**4. Results are listed with real numbers, not adjectives.** A runs eight ✅ lines, each
carrying a percentage. B names six clients with their outcomes, each in the shape
"[client] ➡️ $209K revenue 30-day launch, only $8.6K in costs". The format Catliff prescribes is the same shape:

> BUILT/DELIVERED [what] for [company], achieving [specific result with number] in [timeframe]

**5. Structure comes from Unicode bold, because Upwork strips markdown.** A and B
both write their section headers in 𝗺𝗮𝘁𝗵𝗲𝗺𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗰𝗮𝗹 𝗯𝗼𝗹𝗱 characters; C uses emoji headers
instead. Either works. Plain unformatted paragraphs do not, because nobody reads a wall.

**6. A keyword block near the end, for search rather than for humans.** B is refreshingly
open about it and heads his "𝗞𝗲𝘆𝘄𝗼𝗿𝗱𝘀 𝗛𝘂𝗺𝗮𝗻𝘀 𝗖𝗮𝗻 𝗜𝗴𝗻𝗼𝗿𝗲 🙂". A folds hers into each
section as "Technology Expertise: Klaviyo, ActiveCampaign, …". Same job, different manners.

**7. All 20 skill slots get used.** A 20, C 20, B 15. Skills are a search
surface; leaving them empty is leaving reach on the table.

**8. It closes with a concrete next step, not a pleasantry.** C's is the sharpest because
it asks for something specific: "Send a 2-3 sentence brief: what you're building, your current
stack, and the specific problem you want solved."

## What only some do, so treat as optional

- **Testimonials inside the overview text.** A places a ⭐ quote at the end of each
  service block, with client name and company. B runs two long quotes. C carries
  none and substitutes certifications and named partners. Both routes are represented among
  winners, so pick per person: quotes if they exist and name real people, badges if not.
- **Engagement models and pricing tiers spelled out.** Only C does this ($2K-$15K fixed,
  $50K+ platform builds, $95-125/hr fractional). It fits someone who serves SMB through
  enterprise; it would clutter a narrower profile.
- **An explicit "how I work" week-by-week.** Again only C. Strong for large engagements,
  overkill for small ones.

## What Catliff's formula bans, and the profiles confirm

None of the three contains "I would love to", "I'm excited", "passionate", "results-driven",
"rockstar", or "ninja". None opens by restating the job. None uses a macro-frame closing
("AI is the future of business…"). Any of these appearing in a draft is a defect, not taste.

## Applying this in an audit

Each of the eight numbered points above is a checkable finding. Concretely: does the title
carry pipe-separated keywords a client would search · does line one state an outcome · is the
strongest available proof in the first 250 characters · do result lines carry numbers · is
there visual structure at all · is there a keyword block · are all 20 skill slots filled · is
there a specific next step.

**Do not compare a beginner against these three head-on.** A has 734 jobs and C
1,088,697 dollars behind them; a new profile cannot borrow that and must not pretend to. What
transfers is the *form* — outcome first, numbers over adjectives, structure, keywords, a clear
ask. The proof gets filled with whatever the person genuinely has, which for a new profile may
be outside work entirely. An invented number is worse than a missing one, because the first
call exposes it.
