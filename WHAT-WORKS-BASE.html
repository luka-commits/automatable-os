Half an hour once, then it runs your day.

One conversation, about half an hour. Below is every phase the setup goes through, what each one leaves behind, and what the system does for you every day after that.

4 phases in the base 20 to 30 minutes, once Nothing installed before you say yes

How the pieces fit

This page is the base layer. Add-ons sit on top of it, and you never pick between them.

The base is set up first, and for a lot of people it's all they ever switch on.

An add-on is one channel of work, bolted on during setup when you say you use it. Say no and nothing about it appears anywhere.

The next one docks in the same way, without the base changing. That's the whole point of the seam.

You never download two things and hope they fit. There's one system, and the setup asks which channels you actually work through.

The whole thing, on one picture

A folder on your machine, a chat in front of it, and two places anything ever leaves to. That's all of it.

Connections

through Claude's own settings, never a password

This package brings no connection of its own and never sees a password: you sign in, Claude gets the access. Two ways in, and the setup can drive either one.

Connect in the app, or from the terminal

In the Claude app. Customize → Connectors → Add. The connection belongs to your account, so it's there in the app, in the terminal and in VS Code without doing it again.

Or from the session you're already in, which is the way out when the button is blocked. A work account needs an owner to release a connector once, and the free plan allows exactly one, so this route matters more often than it sounds:

claude mcp add --transport http <name> <url> -s user

Then /mcp, pick it, choose Authenticate. The -s user flag makes the connection follow you instead of the folder. Claude runs this for you, so you don't have to remember the syntax.

Either way: restart the session afterwards. A connection made mid-session is invisible to the session that made it, and that one step is behind most of the "it says it isn't connected" reports.

Why the setup just installs them

A recommendation you have to act on later is a recommendation nobody acts on. The set is installed once, in one question, and every one of them can be removed with a single command afterwards.

The two below it are separated for a reason: claude-mem keeps its own copy of what it saw, which matters if you work with client material, and last30days is somebody else's skill on its own release cycle.

Your own repos

the backup, and any project code
Your backup repocarries your history

Created under your own account. It's private and it's yours from the start. The end of the day pushes the state of this folder into it. Nobody else is offered access to it.

Project reposif any

Cloned into projects/<slug>/code, so the project keeps its own history and doesn't get mixed into the workspace.

What happens if you skip the backup repo

A fresh clone still points at the repo of whoever sent you the package. Without this step the end of the day would push your work, your notes and your client material into their repo, every evening, quietly.

So the setup renames that remote to upstream and creates yours as origin. Updates keep flowing in from upstream; nothing of yours ever flows back out.

A connector that doesn't exist gets said out loud. The catalogue moves in both directions, so a route is never guessed at. A connection that fails twice is recorded as not set up and the setup carries on: the rest does not wait for it.
